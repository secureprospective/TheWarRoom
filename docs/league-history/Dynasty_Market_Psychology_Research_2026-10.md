# Dynasty Market Psychology Through the Calendar Year: Research Pass

Date: 2026-10-05. Written before any analysis of the 14-year MFL league archive, so the
archive can be used to test these claims rather than being read through them.

## How solid is this evidence

- **Hard data (studied):** rookie-pick hit rates; RB/WR age curves; the endowment effect
  in real pro-sports draft-pick trading.
- **Measured market data (one source):** KTC rookie-pick prices tracked across one
  calendar year (Footballguys, "Dynasty Investor").
- **Practitioner consensus (not measured):** almost everything about monthly
  psychology. Everyone repeats the same calendar, but nobody has published
  trade-level data that proves it. The "picks peak at 120% in April" figure is repeated
  across blogs, and none of them gives a source for it.
- **Gap:** no published work on long-running *salary-cap/contract* leagues' calendar
  behaviour. Our archive can fill that gap.

## The calendar

| Window | NFL/league trigger | Market mood | Rises | Falls |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jan–Feb | Fantasy season over, NFL playoffs | Hangover. Champions are attached to the players they won with; losers turn to rebuilding | Upcoming rookie picks | Aging vets, injured players |
| Mar | NFL league year, free agency, Combine | Uncertainty about vets changing teams; enthusiasm for prospects | Picks, prospects | Vets whose landing spot is unknown |
| Apr | NFL Draft | Peak hype: months of mock drafts compressed into 3 days | Current-year picks (peak) | Vets overshadowed by rookies |
| May–Jun | Rookie drafts | **Picks peak on the clock.** Tunnel vision: managers overpay for "their guy." Then the hype moves from the pick to the drafted rookie | Rookies with good landing spots | Last year's rookies ("old news" discount) |
| Jul–Aug | Camps, preseason | Camp-report noise; whoever is mentioned most gets valued highest | Camp-buzz players, 2nd/3rd-year breakout bets | Vets with depth-chart threats; KTC shows picks drift down |
| Sep–Oct | Weeks 1–6 | Overreaction to small samples; contenders and rebuilders not yet sorted | Hot starters, proven vets | **Picks hit their low point**; slow starters, injured players |
| Nov | Trade deadline | Urgency. Contenders overpay for depth because standing still feels like losing | Playoff-schedule starters, RB handcuffs | Future picks (contenders sell them) |
| Dec | Fantasy playoffs | Window locks; eliminated teams sell | Playoff performers | Vets on eliminated teams |

**The single most repeated rule:** buy picks Sep–Nov, sell them Apr–May (at the rookie
draft). KTC tracking confirms the direction of that rule. It also shows **1st-round picks barely
dip in the offseason**, while 2nd and 3rd-round picks swing much harder.

## How draft picks are treated

- **The life of a pick:** it is an abstraction for 2–3 years (priced as a generic
  "mid"). It becomes concrete once standings set its slot. It peaks when it is on the clock,
  and the hype then passes to the player it was used on.
- **Future-pick discount:** about 25% between next year's 1st and the year after's
  (dynastyblueprint framework, not measured). Uncertainty about the draft class is part of
  why.
- **The "mid" mistake:** managers price every future 1st as a mid 1st, even a pick owned
  by the worst team in the league. Picks from bad teams are underpriced, and picks from
  stacked teams are overpriced.
- **Your own picks are worth more to you.** They are insurance: they gain value in exactly
  the year your team collapses (Harstad).
- **What picks actually return:**
  - 2010–17 (NBC Sports, 504 picks, 12-team): 1st round about 48% produce at least one
    useful season, and only about 30% produce more than one. 2nd round about 31%. 3rd
    round and later about 7%. Only 6% of all picks hit as rookies.
  - 2018–23 (Dynasty Nerds): 1.01 about 83%, 1.02–1.04 about 71%, 1.05–1.08 about 38%,
    1.09–1.12 about 46% (the 2020 class skews that last number), early 2nds about 22%.
  - So the market pays for hope. The top 4 picks are much more reliable than the rest,
    and after the 1st round the payoff falls off sharply.
- **Endowment effect (real pro-sports evidence):** Hobbs et al. (2022, NFL/NBA/NHL)
  found teams hold their *original* picks more stubbornly than picks they acquired;
  acquired picks are reportedly 12–15% more likely to be traded again (search-snippet
  level, paper paywalled). AFL study: about 37% of picks change hands before use, low
  picks circulate most, top-5 picks rarely move, and players drafted with traded-in picks
  are kept *longer* (sunk-cost attachment).

## Manager psychology and habits

- **Endowment and loss aversion:** managers value players they own above market and make
  too few trades. They hold busts too long and refuse to "sell low."
- **Anchoring vs recency:** managers are slow to update on long-term evidence and too
  fast to react to last week. The two biases pull against each other, which keeps prices
  swinging.
- **Rookie fever:** managers buy the most exciting possible outcome rather than the
  most likely one. Last year's rookies are discounted once a new class arrives, although
  most second-year breakout bets still fail.
- **Deadline "do something" urgency:** inactivity feels like regression, so deadline
  trades are made out of emotion rather than value.
- **Age cliffs drive selling behaviour:** most RB1 seasons come at ages 23–26 (57% since
  2000). There is roughly a 10% drop in total points from 26 to 27, and since 2016 the
  peak age is about 24.8. WRs peak around 27 and show no significant points-per-game drop
  until about 30. The market sells RBs at 26–27 and WRs at 29.
- **Activity matters:** one data claim (unverified source) says teams making 21+ trades
  in 3 years are 52% likelier to win than teams making 1–3.
- **League lifecycle:** even good leagues churn managers. In one 19-year league, the
  longest run with the same set of managers was 5 years. An inactive or tanking manager
  distorts the market for years, not one season.

## Contract / salary-cap leagues (our format: MFL in-game contracts)

These extra calendar triggers do not exist in standard dynasty:
- **Cut/release deadlines and dead money:** cutting a player costs cap space, so managers
  keep bad contracts (sunk cost). That pressure peaks just before the league's cut
  deadline.
- **Contract expiry, extensions, franchise tags, RFA:** decisions bunch together in the
  offseason window. Players in the final year of a contract become trade chips, or get
  dumped, before that window.
- **Rookie contracts are cheap.** This makes rookie picks worth *more* in cap leagues than
  in standard dynasty, because they combine production with cap room.
- **Cap space as currency:** contenders run out of room at the deadline, and rebuilders
  bank space and take on bad salary in exchange for picks.

## Our league is not the format the research measured

Legacy NFL is 32 teams, IDP and salary cap. Almost all published numbers come from
12-team, offense-only leagues. Treat them as relative patterns (which way values move,
when the swings happen), not as levels:
- **32 teams means a rookie draft roughly 2.7 times as deep.** Because waivers are bare,
  the rookie draft is the main pipeline for new talent, so the 2nd and 3rd rounds matter
  more than the 12-team hit rates suggest. The slot-by-slot hit rates above do not carry
  over directly.
- **IDP is a thinner, less-informed market.** There are few trade-value resources for
  defenders, so IDP managers sell elite defensive vets too cheaply for youth and picks
  (Footballguys IDP). Expect larger mispricing, and slower price discovery, on defensive
  players than on offensive ones.
- **Picks command a premium in IDP dynasty generally.** The usual counter-strategy is to
  buy whatever is in lower demand.

## Hypotheses to test against the 14-year archive

1. Trade volume peaks around the rookie draft and the trade deadline, with a quiet period
   in late Dec–Feb and Jul.
2. Picks move toward rebuilders in Oct–Nov and toward contenders/drafters in Apr–May.
3. In-season, a team's playoff odds predict whether it buys or sells.
4. Original picks are traded less often than acquired picks (endowment effect inside our
   league).
5. Future 1sts trade at the same price regardless of the owner's record (the "mid"
   mistake), so picks from bad teams are underpriced.
6. Picks taken at 1.05 and later, and 2nd-rounders, return far less than they cost.
7. Managers are slow to sell RBs at age 27 or older.
8. Contract leagues add their own peaks: before the cut deadline, around extension and tag
   decisions, and dumps of expiring contracts.
9. Manager turnover and inactivity show up as dead stretches in trade activity, and
   individual managers have their own trading habits.
10. IDP players are traded at lower prices, and their price movements lag offense
    (a thinner market).
11. With 32 teams, the hit rate of 2nd and 3rd-round picks is meaningfully higher than
    the 12-team benchmarks.
