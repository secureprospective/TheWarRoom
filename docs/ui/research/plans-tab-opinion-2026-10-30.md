# Plans tab: second opinion

Plans should be the owner's private thinking desk: a summary of real activity alongside a small, event-based plan. The other tabs remain the source of truth and the only places to execute. Refine the existing three-column Plans screen rather than building another workspace.

## What to pull in

- **Trade Desk:** Pocket trades, with their partner and whether each is still valid; live negotiations, with whose turn it is. Link to the relevant trade or thread, not a copied version of its contents.
- **Market:** Target lanes by need, showing the leading targets; bids clearly labeled ready/not placed, placed/high, or outbid. Show the relevant bid amount and available cap room, not the whole pool.
- **Draft Room:** Picks currently owned, with round and position or projected position; the top of the actual queue in order, plus its count. A note indicator can link to the private scouting report; don't reproduce the board.

These are live summaries, not items a plan must contain. Show empty states when there is nothing to summarize. If a linked trade, target, pick, or player changes or disappears, show that plainly rather than preserving a misleading snapshot.

## Light functions, ranked

1. **Event checklist:** Add, edit, check off and reorder personal steps under a calendar event. A checkmark means the owner marked it done, not that a league transaction occurred.
2. **Priorities:** Order needs and planned steps so the next decision is obvious. This order does not change bid priority or the draft queue.
3. **Links:** Attach a Pocket trade, negotiation, target or pick to a need or step; open its source tab for the details.
4. **Notes:** Add a short private rationale to a plan or step, including a fallback. Keep scouting reports in Draft Room.
5. **Named alternatives:** Create or copy a plan and switch between alternatives without changing live activity.
6. **Event placement:** Move a step to another calendar event; warn if it falls outside a known window, without preventing the owner from recording the thought.

## Current pieces

- **Named plans — keep:** They make alternate approaches legible; selecting one must not change live trades, bids or the draft queue.
- **Steps per calendar event — merge:** Make these the checklist and allow links to live summary items, rather than duplicating their state.
- **Cap outcome — keep, qualified:** Show current room and, only when supported by reliable inputs, a clearly labeled hypothetical plan outcome with assumptions. Don't treat unsigned trades or unplaced bids as completed or double-count linked items; otherwise show unavailable.
- **Conflicts — keep, focused:** Flag known window, cap or asset conflicts, identify the steps involved, and distinguish conditional alternatives from actual contradictions. Don't claim the plan is clean when checks are incomplete.

## One 1920×1080 screen

Use the existing hard-edged three-column frame beneath the tab bar, with no page scroll. Keep compact rows and independent scrolling inside bounded panels when needed.

- **Left, narrow:** Named plan list, active selection and a new/copy control; below it, the owner's ordered needs.
- **Center, widest:** Selected plan's calendar events in chronological order, each with a compact checklist, priority and linked-item labels. Surface the nearest events first; scroll within this frame for the rest.
- **Right:** Three compact live summary sections for Trade Desk, Market and Draft Room, followed by current cap, qualified hypothetical outcome and actionable conflict warnings. Make status and source explicit.
- **Side drawer:** Open a step to edit its note, event and links, or inspect a conflict and its assumptions. Links navigate to the source tab; player and team details use the existing inspector. No execution controls in the drawer.

Use hard borders and high-contrast text, with color as a labeled accent; no glow. The screen should still work if a section is empty or data is unavailable.

## Keep out

- Send, accept or counter trades; place or change bids; draft players; or other league execution controls.
- Editable copies of trade versions, target lanes, bid priority, draft queue, scouting reports or full boards.
- A second calendar, notification center, trade builder, forecasting engine or elaborate project-management system.
- Automatic execution, automatic reprioritization or a checkmark implying a transaction happened.
- Unqualified cap projections, invented conflict certainty, stale status presented as live, and decorative panels that consume space without helping a decision.
