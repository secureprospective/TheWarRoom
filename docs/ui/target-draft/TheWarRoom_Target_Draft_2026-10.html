The visual system: four jobs, four channels

Each channel has one job. Colour says what something is, the frame says how much is at stake, the countdown says how soon, and a monochrome glyph says what kind of thing it is. No channel does two jobs, so you can read a card's importance without reading a word.

Colour = meaning. Five, locked; no theme can change them
GreenElite, positive, landed, cheaper than the league beyond chance
BlueInformation, live now, on the field, selected source
AmberWatch: a clock is running, a check needs you
RedDanger, injury, blocked, under one hour, dearer than the league beyond chance
GreyHistory, closed, stale, not wired yet

Colour never carries position, tier, gravity or team. It always comes with a word or an icon, so it still reads for a colour-blind owner.

Home: “what needs me, and how long do I have?”

The seasonal card owns the page and changes with the league year. The alert tray sorts by urgency, then consequence. Clicking anything fills the inspector on the right and never leaves the page.

1The seasonal card owns Home. The league-year track shows where we are among the eight stretches; the three nearest clocks sit under it. In the offseason the same card shows windows instead of weeks.
2The tray sorts by urgency, then stakes. The offer outranks the lineup gap because it expires in 47 minutes, and its 3px frame says that accepting cannot be undone.
3Every item has one action. “Set lineup” and “Open offer” land you one click from a ready move, inside the two-click rule.
4The inspector is the only entity page. It replaces MFL's franchise hub (25 kinds of links) with zones. Nothing navigates away.
5The digests are league-wide. “Market this week” points at the whole league, not at Arizona, and only colours a line when the gap is beyond chance.
6Every control is a command. The grey line under each inspector names the verb, so the future chat engine can drive the same screen.

Franchise HQ › Lineup and roster: “is my team set, and what does it cost me?”

The roster as a sortable board at Tactical density. Every row carries the two locked numbers, on-field now and dynasty value, next to the contract. Select a row and the player opens in the inspector, with that player's actions in a tray at the bottom.

1The two locked numbers sit side by side: on-field now and dynasty value, then salary, so cost is always next to worth.
2Problems show in the row: the empty LB slot is red with a word; Newton is amber because a clock and a choice are attached.
3Read tables never hold a commit button. Actions live in the inspector's tray, so you can't trigger a move by mis-clicking a row.
4Contracts and cap is the next tab: what-if, dead cap, and the window acts (option, tender, re-sign, buyout, cut) as they open.
5My moves holds every prepared move as an envelope (see Trade Floor), plus your app-local watch list and scratchpad.
6Density switch in the header: Matrix shrinks this to 22px rows for a 48-man sweep; Narrative turns each row into a sentence.

Trade Floor › Trade desk: “is this offer good, and is it safe to say yes?”

An offer is a move envelope. The app checks it, hands you to the exact MFL page, then watches MFL's own data until the trade actually lands. “Ready” means we checked it. Only MFL's data can say it happened.

1The envelope rail is the same for every move. Received or Draft → Checked → Handed off → (league step) → Landed, or Failed, or Stale if nothing shows up in MFL's data.
2Accepting is a hold, not a click, because the 3px frame marks it as irreversible. Reject is a plain click because you can always ask again.
3Phase 1 is honest about MFL: the app prepares and checks, and you finish on MFL. In Phase 2 the hold does the write and nothing else on this screen changes.
4“Against the league” stays neutral here. The gap is within chance, so it says so in grey instead of inventing a verdict.
5DOT review is the league's step after accept. Where MFL shows it is still open question 3; the rail reserves its place.
6Counterparties (the next tab) is each franchise's trading habits, league-wide. Draft room holds pick timing.

War Room › Market: “where is the league mispricing players right now?”

The Lab, rebuilt as a workspace. League-wide, with no Arizona tint. A cell turns green or red only when its gap from the going rate is beyond chance, and every surface has “How this is counted”.

1League-wide by design. Your own team isn't tinted anywhere in the market. You see it in Franchise HQ; here you see the league.
2Decisions, not pictures. Every chart ends in a “so what” line that names a move. The weak signals are labelled weak.
3“Too few” is a state. Under 20 trades, a cell says so instead of guessing.
4The segment opens in the inspector, with its counting rule in plain words and a door to the actual players you could go and buy.
5Valuations and pool holds the asset board and the free-agent pool with bid tracking; your bid and claim plans live in Franchise HQ.
6Built in ring 2. The Lab's JavaScript is ported to the Go engine and must match the Lab's own tests before this screen goes live.

League Pulse › Now: “how is my week going, live?”

The Classic matchup view: the Legacy NFL view, because its long starting lineup doesn't fit a formation. Real NFL games run along the top; the centre spine streams the plays that score fantasy points in this matchup. The Default formation view waits for Christopher's briefing.

1Blue means on the field right now, the way MFL lights players up. Grey means the game is final; the kickoff time means not started.
2The spine is fantasy-first. It shows plays that move this matchup, from any game, whether or not you are following that game.
3The games bar picks the followed game; the inspector shows its field, last play and source. If ESPN drops, Yahoo then Fox take over behind one adapter.
4Points come only from MFL's live scoring. The NFL feeds give play text and game state, never fantasy points.
5Default view (later): the formation laid across the line, your offense against their defense, players lighting up and stats growing beneath them. All leagues get both views; Legacy uses Classic.
6Race is standings, playoff odds and the all-play table. Pools and archive hold every MFL read report in one drawer.

Roadmap to done: built like an onion

Every ring goes through every layer, screen to data contract to data source, and makes the most-used paths deeper first. Each ring ends with a live review on Claude-OS that rewrites this target. Rows that aren't wired yet say “not wired yet”; they never show made-up data. Today's screens are the test harness and are deleted one by one as their replacements go live.
